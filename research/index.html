Research at the intersection of technology, work & migration.

My doctoral research examines how technological transformation changes labour demand and the incentives that shape international migration, including who moves and where.

DISSERTATION / IN PROGRESS

Shifting Grounds

Technological Transformation, Labour Demand, and the Selectivity of Global Migration.

Why do countries with different technological capabilities attract different kinds of migrants? How does automation alter the relative demand for skills? Can widening technological gaps reshape the geography of international mobility?

These questions guide my dissertation, which combines international migration data with cross-country indicators of technological development and labour-market structure.

This page describes an ongoing research project. Hypotheses and conceptual relationships should not be interpreted as established findings.
CENTRAL QUESTIONS
I.

Technology & productive capacity

How do automation, digital connectivity and AI adoption shape the location and scale of economic opportunity?

II.

Demand for different skills

Which tasks are complemented or displaced by technological change, and how does this vary across occupations and countries?

III.

The selectivity of migration

Do international differences in technological capacity change migration flows and the characteristics of those who move?

EMPIRICAL STRATEGY

From questions to evidence

The research develops a cross-country empirical approach drawing on migration flows, technological indicators and labour-market data. Planned methods include gravity-model specifications with high-dimensional fixed effects, along with analyses of heterogeneous migration outcomes by skill.

Research materials and data visualizations will be added as individual projects mature.
